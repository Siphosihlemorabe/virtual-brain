# Learner interview guide

Date: 17 November 2026. Goal: 8–10 interviews, about 20 minutes each.

**What we want to learn:** how people who learn from online courses really study, what goes wrong, and what
they already pay time or money to fix. **Not** whether they like our idea.

## Rules (from "The Mom Test", Rob Fitzpatrick)

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "What did you do last time?" is reliable. "Would you use…?" is not:
   people are polite and optimistic.
2. **Don't pitch until the end**, if at all. Once they know the idea, answers bend towards it.
3. **Dig into specifics.** "Tell me about the last time…", "What happened next?", "Why?"
4. **Listen for money, time and workarounds.** What they already pay for or hack together is the real signal.
5. **Talk less than a third of the time.** Silence after a question is fine.

## Who to talk to

- **Must:** studied from online courses or recorded classes (DataCamp, Udemy, Coursera, YouTube, Zoom) in the
  last 6 months.
- **Ideally:** at least 4 preparing for, or recently sat, an AWS or other cloud certification.
- **Mix:** a couple of online tutors or teachers, for their view on learners.
- **Not:** close friends only. Friends are kind; mix in people from study groups, LinkedIn or Discord communities.

## Script

**Opening (2 min)**
"Thanks for helping. I'm researching how people learn from online courses. There are no right answers, and I'm not
selling anything. Is it OK if I take notes?"

**Context (3 min)**
1. What have you been learning online recently? On which platforms?
2. Why that? Is it for a job, an exam, or curiosity?

**The last time (7 min): the heart of the interview**
3. Think of the last course or certification you studied for. Walk me through a typical study session.
4. After you watched a video, what did you do next, if anything?
5. How did you check whether you'd really understood it?
6. A week later, how much did you remember? When did you notice you'd forgotten something?
7. *(Exam-takers)* How did you decide you were ready for the exam? What happened in it? What surprised you?

**Problems and workarounds (5 min)**
8. What's the most frustrating part of learning this way?
9. Have you tried anything to fix that: apps, notes systems, flashcards, study groups, ChatGPT? What happened?
10. What do you pay for to help you learn (courses, practice exams, tools)? Roughly how much a month?
11. Have you ever stopped using a study tool? Why?

**Wrap-up (3 min)**
12. If you could wave a magic wand over how you study, what would change?
13. Who else should I talk to?
14. *(Only now, optional)* One sentence on the idea: "A browser tool that quizzes you on what you just watched and
    tells you what you're about to forget and whether you're ready for the exam." Then ask: "What would make this
    useless for you?" (Not "would you use it?")

## Notes template (one per interview)

```
Who:            (platform, goal, exam?)
Study habits:   (what they actually do after a video)
Checks understanding by:
Forgetting:     (when they noticed, how bad)
Exam readiness: (how they decided; outcome)
Pain, in their words:  "..."
Workarounds / tools tried:
Spending:       (money / time)
Strongest quote:
Surprises:
```

## What would count as a signal

Decide before interviewing; review after 8–10.

| Signal | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Describe forgetting or "not knowing if I'm ready" as a problem **without being prompted** | at least 5 of 10 |
| Already pay for practice exams or study tools | at least 4 of 10 |
| Have tried and dropped a quiz or flashcard tool | Note **why**. It tells us about the adherence risk |
| Mostly rewatch or reread after videos | Expected (Karpicke et al., 2009). Confirms the gap we'd fill |

## After the interviews

Put the notes side by side and look for repeated pains, the exact words people use (useful for the landing page),
and what they pay for. Then decide with the self-test results: build as specced, move toward the exam coach, or
rethink.
